# Reading the reports: what each number means

> The search terms report tells you **what people typed**. The reports on this page tell you **whether your ads could show, what Google thinks of them, and who else was there**. Every example uses County's own account, 1 Oct – 6 Nov 2026.

## 1. Impression share: did we show up?

Think of a shop at a mela. **Impression share** is the share of the people walking past who actually saw your stall.

- Every time someone searches a word you bid on, you are **eligible** to show.
- **Impression share (IS)** = the times you showed ÷ the times you were eligible.
- *Example:* Ivory Brand had about 79% IS. Of every 100 searches it could have shown on, it showed on about 79.

The 21 it missed are split into two reasons, and the difference between them decides what you do next:

| Column | Plain meaning | What fixes it |
|---|---|---|
| **Search lost IS (budget)** | The day's money ran out before the searches stopped | More budget, or spend the same budget on fewer, better searches |
| **Search lost IS (rank)** | You lost the auction: bid × quality was too low | Better ads and landing page (cheaper), or a higher bid (dearer) |

> **Rule of thumb:** never approve a budget increase for a campaign whose "lost IS (budget)" is near zero. The money will not be spent. Ivory CTM lost 0% to budget and 57–80% to rank every day from 29 Oct, so a ₹5k budget would change nothing.

**Top IS** and **Absolute top IS** show how often you were in the top block of ads, and how often you were the very first ad. On brand searches we want the very first slot almost every time, because someone typing "ivory county" is our buyer.

**"< 10%" and "> 90%":** Google hides exact values at the extremes. The dashboard leaves them blank instead of guessing.

### Eligible searches: a number you can work out yourself
Impressions ÷ impression share = how many searches the campaign could have entered.
*Example:* Ivory CTM, 1–8 Oct: about **15,800 a day**. 26 Oct – 4 Nov: about **190 a day**. The campaign did not get worse at winning. It was given far fewer searches to compete for, because its keywords were narrowed (generic keywords switched to exact match on 10 Oct; search term matching turned off on 16 Oct).

## 2. Quality Score: what Google thinks of each keyword

Every keyword gets a score from **1 to 10**. Google grades three things:

1. **Expected click-through rate:** will people click this ad for this search?
2. **Ad relevance:** do the ad's words match the keyword? *"3 BHK in Sector 115, Noida"* in the ad for the keyword *3 bhk flats sector 115* = relevant.
3. **Landing page experience:** when they click, is the page fast, relevant, and easy to use on a phone?

Each part is rated *Above average*, *Average* or *Below average*.

**Why it matters in rupees:** what you pay per click depends on the next advertiser's Ad Rank divided by your quality. Better quality means a lower price for the same position. County's keyword report shows:

- **57% of keyword spend (₹8.14 lakh of ₹14.32 lakh)** went to keywords whose landing page is rated *below average*.
- **₹2.31 lakh** went to keywords with Quality Score **1**, the lowest possible.

The landing page is the part County controls directly, so it is the cheapest lever in the account.

## 3. Conversions: what Google counts is not what Sales counts

- **Conversions** = the actions set as the main goal (usually a lead form or call).
- **All conv.** = those plus secondary actions (page views, clicks on a phone number, and so on).
- *Example:* Ivory Non-brand shows **20** conversions but **6,741** all conversions. Something other than leads is being counted in "All conv.". Confirm what it is before anyone quotes that number.
- **Cost / conv.** compares campaigns with each other. It says nothing about lead **quality**. Only the CRM knows if a lead was qualified, visited the site, or booked.

## 4. Bid strategies in one line each

| Strategy | What you tell Google | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Maximise clicks | "Get as many clicks as the budget allows" | Cheap clicks are often junk clicks |
| Maximise conversions | "Get as many conversions as the budget allows" | Spends the whole budget every day |
| Target CPA | "Get conversions at about ₹X each" | Too low a target makes Google stop bidding (Ivory CTM) |
| Target impression share | "Show on X% of searches, whatever it costs" | Can pay very high CPCs (Cocoa Brand: about ₹240 a click) |

After any change to a target or strategy, Google re-learns for about **1–2 weeks**. Judge the change after that, not the next morning.

## 5. Auction insights: who else is in the room

| Column | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| Impression share | How often that advertiser showed on searches we could show on |
| Overlap rate | How often they showed in the **same** auction as us |
| Position above rate | When we met, how often their ad was above ours |
| Outranking share | How often we were above them, or showed when they did not |

County, 1–6 Nov: **99acres** showed on 47% of our searches and sat above us 54% of the time we met. **housing.com** showed on 26%. Portals are a fact of property search. Ask for this report **per campaign**, so brand and non-brand are not mixed.

## 6. Status notes Google writes on campaigns and keywords

| Note | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Limited by budget | Lost IS (budget) is high on some days |
| Bidding strategy limited | The target (CPA or impression share) is holding the campaign back |
| Missing enough relevant keywords | The ad group has too few keywords that get searches |
| Rarely served | The keyword almost never wins an auction (low quality or too narrow) |
| Low quality | Quality Score is low; the keyword rarely shows and pays more when it does |
| Some ads disapproved | At least one ad broke a policy and is not running |

## 7. Which report answers which question

| Question | Report |
|---|---|
| What did people type, and should we block it? | Search terms report |
| Did the agency do what the sheet says? | Change history |
| Is the campaign held back by budget or by rank? | Campaign report with impression-share columns |
| Which keywords are expensive because of low quality? | Keyword report with Quality Score columns |
| Who are we competing against? | Auction insights |
| Were the leads any good? | CRM (not in Google Ads) |

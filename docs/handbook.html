Part 1 · Google Ads from zero

What actually happens when someone searches

1. The auction

  1. A person types something into Google, for example 3 bhk flat in noida. What they typed is the search term.
  2. In a fraction of a second Google runs an auction among every advertiser who said they want searches like that.
  3. Positions are decided by Ad Rank: how much you are willing to pay (your bid) combined with how good and relevant Google thinks your ad and page are (quality). A very relevant ad can beat a bigger bid.
  4. The winners show. You pay only when someone clicks (cost per click, CPC), usually a little less than your bid: roughly what you needed to beat the advertiser below you.
Remember this one

Google lets one ad per advertiser into each auction. If two of our campaigns want the same search, only the one with the higher Ad Rank takes part. Our own campaigns can block each other.

3. Keyword vs search term: the most important idea

  • A keyword is what we give Google: "show my ad for searches like this".
  • A search term is what the person actually typed.
  • They are rarely identical. Google decides which searches are close enough, using the keyword's match type.
From our account, 5 Nov

Our keyword "noida premium apartments" (Clove non-brand) showed an ad for the search skyline suites noida, another builder's project. One click cost ₹2,286. Nobody typed our keyword; Google decided it was close enough.

4. Match types: how loosely Google may match

Match typeWritten asGoogle may show the ad for
Broadluxury flats noidaAnything related in meaning, even without those words. Can match other builders' project names.
Phrase"luxury flats in noida"Searches that include the meaning of the phrase, with words around it.
Exact[luxury flats in noida]Searches with the same meaning, e.g. noida luxury flats.

"(close variant)" in the search terms report means Google matched what it considers the same meaning: plurals, misspellings, word order, synonyms (flat and apartment), sometimes dropped words. Most of our junk comes in through phrase-match close variants.

5. Negative keywords: "never show for this"

A negative stops an ad when the search contains it. Negatives use the same three match types, but they are strict: they do not stretch to plurals, misspellings or synonyms.

Danger

A single common word as a campaign-wide broad negative silently kills good traffic. -62 blocks sector 62 gurgaon; -home blocks luxury home in noida.

6. Many ad groups and "funnelling"

Ivory non-brand has nine ad groups (Flats, Apartments, Projects, Villa, House, Home, Pent House, Property, Societies). Google treats flat, apartment, home and house as near-synonyms, so one search can be picked up by several of them. Adding ad-group negatives so each group takes only its own word is called query funnelling. It is a real technique, and it only works at ad-group level. Done carelessly, it blocks the group's own keywords.

From our account

Ivory sells no villas and no penthouses, yet the Villa and Pent House ad groups cost ₹10,361 with 0 conversions between 1 Oct and 6 Nov. Every click there was someone looking for a product we cannot sell them.

7. Bidding: what "target CPA" really does

  • Conversion: an action we count as success, here mostly a lead form.
  • CPA (cost per acquisition) = spend ÷ conversions. ₹10,000 for 2 leads = ₹5,000 CPA.
  • Target CPA: we tell Google "aim for leads at this cost". Google then bids, for each search, roughly target CPA × the chance this click becomes a lead.
  • Budget is the most a campaign may spend per day. It only limits a campaign that actually reaches it.
  • Learning period: after a big bidding change, allow about 1–2 weeks before judging it.

Try it: what can Google afford to bid?

Roughly ₹60 per click.
From our account: Ivory CTM

Since 1 Oct, CTM turned about 7.5% of its clicks into Google conversions (30 of 398). At a ₹800 target that allows roughly ₹60 a click; in November it actually paid about ₹26 on average. The agency's Ivory Brand campaign pays ₹200–₹500 a click for the same ivory county searches, so on those searches it wins the auction over CTM. The bigger cause: after its keywords were narrowed in October, the searches CTM could even enter fell from about 15,800 a day to about 190. Raising its budget would change nothing.

8. Conversions in Google vs leads in the CRM

Google counts a conversion when the form is submitted. The CRM later marks the lead Qualified, Disqualified or Pending. A search that "converted" can still be a bad lead, so a converting search term is not automatically a keeper. Check lead quality before deciding.

Part 2 · Reading the reports

What each number in the reports means

The search terms report says what people typed. These reports say whether our ads could show, what Google thinks of them, and who else was there. Examples are from 1 Oct – 6 Nov 2026.

9. Impression share: did we show up?

Think of a stall at a mela. Impression share is the share of people walking past who actually saw your stall: the times you showed ÷ the times you were eligible.

Rule of thumb

Never approve a budget increase for a campaign whose "lost IS (budget)" is near zero. The extra money will not be spent.

A number you can work out yourself

Impressions ÷ impression share = how many searches a campaign could have entered. If that falls, the campaign has fewer searches to compete for, and no budget or bid fixes it. Google hides extreme values as "< 10%" or "> 90%"; leave those out rather than guess.

10. Quality Score: what Google thinks of each keyword

Every keyword gets a score from 1 to 10, built from three ratings (Above average, Average, Below average):

  1. Expected click-through rate: will people click this ad for this search?
  2. Ad relevance: do the ad's words match the keyword? "3 BHK in Sector 115, Noida" in the ad for 3 bhk flats sector 115 is relevant.
  3. Landing page experience: is the page fast, on topic and easy to use on a phone?

Better quality means a lower price for the same position.

From our account

57% of keyword spend (₹8.14 L of ₹14.32 L) went to keywords whose landing page Google rates below average. ₹2.31 L went to keywords with Quality Score 1, the lowest possible. The landing page is the part County controls directly, which makes it the cheapest lever in the account.

Conversions vs "All conv."

Conversions are the main goal (lead form or call). All conv. adds secondary actions such as page views. Ivory Non-brand shows 20 conversions but 6,741 all conversions, so something other than leads is counted there. Confirm what before anyone quotes it.

Part 3 · The STR SOP

The daily search-terms review

14. What an SOP is, and why we need one

An SOP (Standard Operating Procedure) is a written recipe for a job that repeats. It says who does what, when, with which inputs, by which rules, and how we check it was done. It exists so that:

  • the job is done the same way every day, whoever does it;
  • mistakes are caught by a check, not by luck;
  • "why did we exclude this?" has a written answer, with a date and a person;
  • the agency's work is measured against an agreed standard instead of opinions.

A good SOP is short, uses numbered steps and fixed rules wherever possible, and changes only through a version note.

Purpose of this one: stop paying for clicks from people who will not buy a County home, without cutting the people who would. Scope: all agency-run search campaigns for Ivory, Clove, Cocoa and Jade. Google's Ivory CTM campaign is watched, and findings go to Google's team through County.

16. Decision rules for one search term

Ask in order and stop at the first answer.

1

Wrong campaign type? Brand campaign but no brand word (county, clove, cocoa, ivory, jade) → Exclude the non-brand part. Non-brand or competitor campaign with our brand word → Exclude here; the brand campaign should own it.

Example: ats pious hideaways sector 150 noida in the Clove brand campaign. Clove starts at ₹8 Cr; this is another builder's buyer.

2

Someone who lives there, not someone buying? House or flat number, block, pin code, "near me", tower number → Exclude.

3

Renting or staying? Rent, PG, 1 RK, studio, serviced apartment, furnished, sharing, Airbnb → Exclude.

4

Budget clearly below ours? Lakhs, "under 1 crore", affordable, cheap, 1 or 2 BHK → Exclude. Starting prices: Jade ₹2.71 Cr, Ivory ₹3.8 Cr, Cocoa ₹4.14 Cr, Clove ₹8 Cr.

5

Another builder's project? In a non-brand campaign → Exclude the project name. In a competitor campaign → keep only if its starting price is within ours ± ₹50 lakh and its location is one we accept.

6

Wrong area? A different city or a far area for that project → Exclude the area name.

7

A real buyer search that fits the product? A configuration the project has (Ivory 3/4/5 BHK, Jade 3/4/5 BHK, Cocoa 3/4 BHK), luxury, our sector or corridor, ready to move, new launch → Add as a phrase keyword in the right ad group. Villa and penthouse searches are never an Add for Ivory.

8

Relevant but general, no signal? flats in noida, noida flat price, Hinglish without budget or size → Leave. Adding them would pull in cheap broad traffic; excluding them would cut volume.

Write "Leave", never blank

A blank Status cell cannot tell "decided to leave" apart from "nobody looked".

17. How to write the negative

  1. Use the smallest negative that removes the junk. The project name as a phrase ("skyline suites") beats one word (skyline).
  2. Never use a bare number (-62) or a single common property word (home, house, flat, project, society, sector, city, new, greater) as a campaign-wide broad negative.
  3. Pick the level on purpose and write it in the sheet: ad group, campaign or shared list.
  4. Junk that fits every project goes in the shared list. hans residency was excluded in Ivory, then cost ₹830 in Clove.
  5. Check before applying that the negative does not block one of our own keywords or an Add in the same sheet.
  6. Do not add the same block twice. On 5 Nov, 160 of 162 exact negatives were already covered by negatives added the evening before.

18. Daily QC checklist (about 15 minutes)

  1. Load data: the search terms report, the change history and the agency sheet. The agency tab's spend matches Google's report for that day.
  2. Command centre: read "Decisions waiting", red first.
  3. Daily STR review: filter "Flagged, no call" and comment on each row.
  4. Said vs done: chase every call older than the service level.
  5. Account risks: brand leakage both ways, keywords blocked by our own negatives, risky negatives.
  6. Weekly: campaign and keyword reports, then Campaign health and Keyword quality.
